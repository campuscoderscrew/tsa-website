## Objective

A visitor on `/join` can tell TSA they're interested (name, email, maybe a
couple of optional details) and gets a clear confirmation. Submissions reach the
club in a place the club controls, and keep reaching them after this year's
developers graduate.

## Background

`features_TODO.md` flags this as the decision to make early: *"A contact form
needs a backend, which a static GitHub Pages site doesn't have. Options:
Formspree, Google Forms embed, or a `mailto:`."* The club hasn't answered
open question 3 yet: "Do they want a contact form, or is email and Instagram
enough?"

The realistic options:

| Option | How it works | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Link to a Google Form** | A button opens a form the club owns | Leaves the site, but the club already knows Google Forms and owns the responses |
| **Embed a Google Form** | An `<iframe>` on the page | Fixed height, scrolling inside scrolling on phones, the embedded form's accessibility is out of our hands |
| **Form service** (Formspree or similar) | Our own `<form>` posts to a third-party endpoint that emails the club | Account ownership, free-tier limits, spam |
| **`mailto:` link** | Opens the visitor's email app | Many phones have no mail app set up; nothing is captured if they bail |

Two things matter more than which one looks nicest:

- **Ownership.** Whatever account receives submissions must belong to the club
  (for example `umdtsa@gmail.com`), not to a student developer. Otherwise the
  form silently dies the year that developer graduates.
- **Privacy.** We'd be collecting students' names and emails. Collect the
  minimum, say what it's for, and never store submissions in this repo.

## Specification

1. **Decide with the club, then write it down.** Present the options table to
   the club (Alison Wang, awang527@terpmail.umd.edu) with your recommendation,
   and ask which account should own submissions. Record the decision, who made
   it, and why in the PR. If you get no answer this week, ship the **Google Form
   link** option only if the club already has a form; otherwise ship the W38.6
   contact link as the CTA and leave this spec `Blocked` with the question
   written out.

2. **Keep the settings in content, not code.** The form URL or endpoint, and
   the privacy line, live in `src/content/join.ts`, so a future officer can
   change them without editing a component.

3. **If you link out:** the CTA is a clearly labeled link ("Sign up on Google
   Forms", so the visitor knows they'll leave the site) that opens in a new tab
   with `rel="noreferrer"`.

4. **If you build a form,** it must:
   - ask for the minimum: name and email required; anything else (year,
     major, how they heard about TSA) optional
   - use real `<label>`s, correct `type` and `autocomplete` attributes, and
     the browser's built-in validation as the first line of defense
   - show errors in text next to the field, tied to it with
     `aria-describedby`, and move focus to the first invalid field on submit
   - show a success message that is announced to screen readers, and doesn't
     wipe what the visitor typed if the submission fails
   - disable the submit button while sending, so a double tap doesn't send twice
   - include a simple spam measure the service supports (a honeypot field is
     the usual one)
   - show one line saying what the information is used for and who sees it

5. **No secrets in the client.** Anything in the bundle is public. A form
   endpoint id is fine to ship; an API key is not. If an option needs a secret,
   it doesn't work on a static site.

6. **Test a real submission end to end** on `npm run preview`, and confirm with a
   club officer that it arrived where they expected. Then delete the test entry,
   or ask them to.

## Out of Scope

- A general contact form for questions (the email link from W38.6 covers that)
- Storing submissions anywhere in this repo
- A newsletter or mailing list (`features_TODO.md`)
- Payments for dues

## Hints

- Free-tier limits and features for form services change. Check the current
  pricing page before recommending one, and quote the limit you found, with the
  date, in the PR.
- `autocomplete="name"` and `autocomplete="email"` let phones fill the form in
  one tap.
- A honeypot is a field hidden from humans (visually and from assistive tech)
  that bots fill in. If it's only visually hidden, screen-reader users will
  find it and fill it in too.
- `fetch` to a form endpoint with `Accept: application/json` usually gets you a
  JSON response you can use for the success state, instead of a redirect.
- Read WebAIM's article on accessible form validation before you design your
  error messages.
