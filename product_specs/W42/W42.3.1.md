## Objective

The About section stops saying "Placeholder" on the live site and says what TSA
actually is and does, in words the club approved. After this lands, a club
officer can change that copy without touching a component.

## Background

TSA asked for an **"About Section."** One exists, and it's the most visibly
unfinished part of the site. From `features_TODO.md`:

- All four cards in `AboutUsScroll.tsx` have literal placeholder body text
  ("Placeholder text about cultural celebrations, food nights, and shared
  traditions…"), with the word **"Placeholder" visible on the live site**.
- The eyebrow says **"Our Services"**: startup language for a student club.
- The heading reads "Built for culture, community, and impact."
- The subheading describes the animation to the visitor ("each section pins,
  then tilts and scales away as the next one arrives"). That's a developer
  describing their own work.

The card headings (Cultural Events, Community, Leadership, Support) are fine.

This is blocked on the club, not on engineering (`features_TODO.md`, open
question 8: "Is anyone available to write the About section copy, or should we
draft and have them edit?"). Drafting it yourself and asking them to edit is
faster than waiting for a blank page to be filled.

TSA's own description of itself, from the intake form: *a fun and welcoming
organization focused on celebrating Thai culture … a festive spirit that is
inviting to everyone.* Their Instagram shows what that means in practice:
Songkran, Loy Krathong, Thai trivia, dessert fundraisers, GBMs, mixers with
other Asian student orgs.

The section uses GSAP `ScrollTrigger` to pin each card, then tilt and scale it
away. Card height depends on text length, and pinning measures heights.

## Specification

1. **Draft the copy, then get it approved.** Write a draft of the eyebrow, the
   heading, a one-sentence subheading, and a short body (two or three
   sentences) for each of the four cards. Send it to Alison Wang
   (awang527@terpmail.umd.edu) on day 1, asking the club to correct or replace
   anything. Paste the message in the PR.

2. **Don't state facts you can't source.** No founding year, member count,
   meeting time or event name that isn't on TSA's Instagram or confirmed by the
   club. "We host GBMs throughout the semester" is safe. "We meet every
   Tuesday at 7pm in Stamp" is only safe if they told you. Mark anything
   unconfirmed with `// TODO: unconfirmed` and list it in the PR.

3. **Move the copy into `src/content/about.ts`.** Export the eyebrow, heading,
   subheading and the four cards (title and body) as typed data. The component
   renders from it. Add a comment at the top saying a non-developer can edit
   the strings, and what they must not change (the keys, the quotes).

4. **Write for the visitor.** The eyebrow names the section, not a business
   offering. The subheading says something about the club, not the animation.
   Keep the tone the brief asks for: fun but professional.

5. **Keep the animation working with the new lengths.** Check that the pinned
   cards don't overlap, clip text or leave a jump at 375px, 768px and 1440px.
   If the card heights changed, check whether `ScrollTrigger` needs a refresh
   after fonts load.

6. **No Thai script without fonts.** If the club wants a Thai word or phrase in
   the copy, Fraunces and Manrope have no Thai glyphs and the browser will fall
   back to an unstyled system font. Don't add it in this spec. Add it to
   `features_TODO.md` under "Thai script support," with the phrase they asked for.

7. **Leave the card headings and order alone** unless the club asks to change
   them.

## Out of Scope

- Translating the section

## Hints

- Short beats long. Each card is read while it's pinned and moving; two
  sentences is plenty.
- `ScrollTrigger.refresh()` recalculates pin positions. Fonts arriving after
  first paint can change text height; `document.fonts.ready` is a promise you
  can wait on.
- Read the copy aloud. If a sentence sounds like a company website, rewrite it.
- `as const` on the content object keeps the strings exact; a typed interface
  gives a clearer error if someone deletes a field. Pick whichever error message
  a non-developer would rather read.
