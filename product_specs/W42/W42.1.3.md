## Objective

The events page gains a month view: a familiar calendar grid where a visitor
can see at a glance what's happening in November, step to the next month, and
open an event. It's the literal **"calendar"** TSA asked for, and it has to work
for keyboard and screen-reader users as well as it works for a mouse.

## Background

After W42.1.1 and W42.1.2 the events page has an agenda (upcoming) and an
archive (past). Both are lists. A list is the better format on a phone and for
a screen reader. But a month grid answers a question a list doesn't: "what's
going on around the week of my midterm?" It's also what people mean when they
say "calendar."

A calendar grid is one of the most common places a site becomes unusable with
a keyboard. Typical failures:

- every day is a tab stop, so getting past one month takes 30+ presses of Tab
- days with events look clickable but are `<div>`s
- the grid is a pile of `<div>`s, so a screen reader reads 42 unlabeled numbers
- at 375px, seven columns become 45px wide and the event titles disappear

## Specification

1. **Choose the accessibility model before you write any markup**, and write
   your choice in the PR. Two defensible options:

   - **A `<table>` with events as links.** The grid is a real table with a
     `<caption>` ("November 2026") and `<th>` weekday headers. Days are plain
     cells. Only days with events contain focusable links, so Tab moves from
     event to event, never from empty day to empty day.
   - **An interactive grid** following the WAI-ARIA grid pattern: one tab stop
     into the grid, arrow keys move between days (roving `tabindex`),
     Home/End and Page Up/Down behave as the pattern describes.

   The first is simpler and probably right for a page whose job is reading. The
   second is what a date *picker* needs. Don't build the second unless you can
   say why the first isn't enough.

2. **Build `MonthCalendar`.** It takes a month (`YYYY-MM`) and a list of
   events, and renders a seven-column month. Weeks start on **Sunday** (US
   convention). Leading and trailing days from the neighboring months are
   either blank or visibly muted, and never carry events. A month can need six
   rows; don't assume five.

3. **Show events in their day cells.** Each event shows its title (truncated if
   it must be, with the full title available to assistive tech). A day with
   more events than fit shows "+2 more" that leads to the full list. A
   multi-day event (`end` set) appears on every day it covers.

4. **Mark today.** Today's cell is visually distinct and has
   `aria-current="date"`. "Today" is computed in the visitor's browser.

5. **Month navigation.** Previous / next buttons with accessible names
   ("Previous month", "Next month"), plus a "Today" button that jumps back to
   the current month. The visible month lives in the URL (`?month=2026-11`) and
   survives a refresh. When the month changes, announce the new month once.

6. **Opening an event.** Activating an event goes to its Instagram post if it
   has one, or scrolls to and focuses the event in the agenda/archive list
   below. Pick one behavior and keep it consistent.

7. **Add the view toggle.** The page offers "List" and "Month" views. Put the
   view in the URL alongside W42.1.2's parameters. List stays the default.

8. **Make 375px work.** Seven readable columns don't fit on a phone. Decide
   what happens: dots in the cells with the day's events listed below the grid
   when a day is selected, an automatic switch to list view below a breakpoint,
   or something else. Whatever you choose, nothing is cut off and nothing
   scrolls sideways.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Accessibility model chosen and justified in the PR
- [ ] Month grid starts on Sunday, handles 4-, 5- and 6-row months (check February 2026, November 2026 and August 2026)
- [ ] Leading/trailing days are blank or muted and never show events
- [ ] Multi-day events appear on every day they cover
- [ ] Today has `aria-current="date"` and a visible marker
- [ ] Previous / next / today work; month is in the URL and survives refresh; month change is announced once
- [ ] Keyboard: getting from the first event to the last in a month never requires tabbing through empty days (or, if you built the grid pattern, arrow keys behave as APG describes)
- [ ] **Screen reader** (VoiceOver or NVDA): the month name, weekday headers and each event's date are announced. Write down what you heard in the PR.
- [ ] At 375px nothing is cut off or scrolls horizontally; screenshot of your mobile approach
- [ ] View toggle works and its state is in the URL
- [ ] No brand hex; today's marker and event chips meet AA contrast

## Out of Scope

- A date picker for jumping to an arbitrary month

- Google Calendar sync

## Hints

- Building the grid is plain arithmetic: find the weekday of the 1st, step back
  to Sunday, then generate 7 × N days. Do it on `YYYY-MM-DD` strings or
  with `Date.UTC`, and never mix local and UTC dates in the same calculation.
- `new Date(Date.UTC(y, m + 1, 0)).getUTCDate()` gives the number of days in a
  month.
- APG pages to read: "Grid Pattern" and the "Date Picker Dialog" example (to
  see what the interactive model costs). For the table model, read up on
  `<caption>` and `scope="col"`.
- `aria-label` on a link that visually says "Trivia" can include the date
  ("Trivia Night, Thursday, March 12"), so a screen-reader user tabbing between
  events knows where they are.
- Don't hide the truncated title from everyone with `display: none`. Visually
  truncate with CSS and let the accessible name carry the full text.
