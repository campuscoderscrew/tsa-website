## Objective

Make each board card say who the person is and what they do, using the data from
W38.3.1 — so the board section stops being a wall of anonymous faces.

## Background

The current card is a square photo with a small lowercase pill in the corner
reading `alison`, `ei`, `linh`. A visitor learns a first name and nothing else.
If they're a prospective member wondering who to email about joining, the section
is useless to them.

TSA's own board cards on Instagram set the target: the person's **role** is the
loudest element after the photo — set vertically down the side in caps
(`PRESIDENT`, `TREASURER`, `SENIOR ADVISOR`, `GRAPHICS COORDINATOR`) — with the
full name beneath and their major under that. It's a strong, repeatable layout,
and it's *theirs*.

You don't have to copy it literally. You do have to make role visible at a glance.

## Specification

1. **Show name and role on every card**, sourced from `BOARD_MEMBERS`. Role
   should be clearly subordinate to the photo but immediately readable — a
   visitor scanning the grid should be able to find "President" without hovering,
   clicking or squinting.

2. **Show major too, if it fits without crowding.** Fourteen cards in a
   four-column grid means each card is around 200px wide on desktop. Three lines
   of text under a square photo at that width gets tight fast. If major doesn't
   fit cleanly, leave it for the W38.3.3 overlay and say so in the PR. Knowing
   what to cut is part of the work.

3. **Keep the existing character.** The scattered rotation, the hover inertia,
   the soft shadows — that playfulness matches TSA's "fun but professional"
   brief and it's the best thing about the current section. This is an
   information upgrade, not a replacement. If your redesign flattens the cards
   into a neat corporate grid, you've overshot.

4. **Rework the name tag.** The current pill sits in the top-left corner and
   overlaps the photo edge. With a role added, one pill won't carry it. Consider:
   a caption block below the photo, an overlay band along the bottom, or role set
   vertically along one edge as TSA does. Try at least two and keep the better
   one — put both screenshots in the PR.

5. **Full names, not just nicknames.** Currently the tag shows a lowercase first
   name. The card should now show the person's full name. The nickname can stay
   as a secondary treatment if it reads warmly — that's a judgment call, and
   "alison" in lowercase does have charm. Decide deliberately.

6. **Contrast is a hard requirement.** Text over a photo needs a scrim, a solid
   backing, or a text shadow — never raw text on an arbitrary photograph. The
   fourteen photos have wildly different backgrounds and brightnesses. Whatever
   treatment you pick must hold up on the **lightest** and the **darkest** photo
   in the set. Find those two, test on them, and screenshot both.

   Target WCAG AA: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text.

7. **Fix the alt text.** Every board photo currently has `alt=""`, which tells a
   screen reader to skip it entirely. These are photos of named people —
   meaningful content. Each needs a real alt like `"Alison Wang, President"`.

   (`alt=""` is correct for genuinely decorative images. It is wrong here.)

8. **Check the grid at every breakpoint.** It currently runs 2 columns → 3 at
   `sm` → 4 at `md`. Adding text changes card height, and uneven text lengths
   ("President" vs "Graphics Coordinator") will make rows ragged. Decide whether
   cards should be equal height and implement it deliberately.

9. **Update the section subheading.** It currently reads "A scattered
   'work-style' grid with inertia scroll. We'll swap these placeholders for real
   board photos + roles next." That's a developer note visible to the public. It
   should now be a sentence written for a visitor — something about who these
   people are and that the club is welcoming.

## Hints

- The inertia effect is a GSAP `quickTo` setup in a `useLayoutEffect` inside
  `HoverInertiaCard`. Read that whole function before restructuring the JSX — the
  effect targets specific refs and will break silently if you move elements
  between the outer and inner div.
- Note the media query at 768px that detaches the pointer handlers on mobile.
  It's there because inertia fights vertical scroll on touch. Don't remove it.
- A `line-clamp` utility handles long roles gracefully. Truncating with an
  ellipsis is acceptable if the full role appears in the W38.3.3 overlay.
- Uneven text lengths breaking grid rows is what CSS Grid's `align-items` and
  `grid-auto-rows` exist for. Worth ten minutes of reading.
- For text on photos, a bottom-anchored gradient scrim (`from-black/70` to
  transparent) is the standard solution and works across every photo brightness.
  The hero already does exactly this — go read how.
- Write alt text as if describing the photo to someone over the phone. "Alison
  Wang, President" is good. "Image of board member" is not.
