## Objective

Put the ornament kit to work on the page so the site visibly reads as Thai, and
replace the generic decoration it's standing in for — without tipping past
"colorful but not overwhelming."

## Background

W38.2.1 built ornaments nobody sees. This is the spec where the site starts
looking like TSA's.

Three sections currently open the same way — a small pill-shaped label
("Our Services", "Events", "Board"), then a heading, then a paragraph. It's
consistent, which is good, and completely generic, which is the problem. That
repeated header is the highest-leverage place to introduce the motif: one change
propagates across the whole page.

You also have two pieces of existing decoration to make a judgment call about:
the blurred red and navy blobs in `BoardMembersSection.tsx`, and the abstract
scribble and blob in `UpcomingEvents.tsx`.

## Specification

1. **Add a `LaiThaiDivider` under each section's eyebrow label.** The three
   sections to treat are About (`AboutUsScroll.tsx`), Events
   (`UpcomingEvents.tsx`) and Board (`BoardMembersSection.tsx`). Same treatment,
   same spacing rhythm in all three — the value here is repetition.

2. **Color it per surface.** Events sits on navy; About and Board sit on sand.
   Gold reads well on navy; on sand it can disappear. Pick per-surface colors
   that hold up, and use the tokens from W38.1.1 if they've landed. Check the
   sand case at arm's length from the screen — a gold hairline on cream is the
   most likely thing to vanish.

3. **Frame the Board section heading with `ThaiCorner`.** Two corners, diagonally
   opposite, is usually more elegant than all four. Try both; keep what looks
   better and say which in the PR.

4. **Add `PrangSilhouette` as a watermark behind one section.** Exactly one.
   Very low opacity — start around 4–6% and adjust. Large, anchored to an edge,
   bleeding off the canvas rather than floating centered. It should be something
   a visitor notices on the second scroll, not the first.

   It must not reduce text contrast anywhere it sits behind copy. If it does,
   move it rather than fading it further into invisibility.

5. **Decide what happens to the existing blobs.** In `BoardMembersSection.tsx`
   there are two large blurred SVG blobs. Options: keep them (they add warmth),
   remove them (they're generic), or keep them at lower opacity behind the new
   prang. There's no single right answer — make a call, implement it, and justify
   it in one sentence in the PR. A reviewer will push back on "I left it because
   I wasn't sure."

6. **Leave the `UpcomingEvents` scribble and blob alone** unless they actively
   clash with what you add. That section has the most going on already; adding
   ornament there needs the most restraint.

7. **Everything you add is `aria-hidden` and `pointer-events-none`.** The
   ornaments already carry `aria-hidden` from W38.2.1, but positioned decorative
   elements can still intercept clicks. Any absolutely-positioned ornament needs
   `pointer-events-none`. Test by trying to click the "View gallery" button with
   an ornament overlapping it.

8. **Respect reduced motion if you animate anything.** The site has
   `animate-tsa-float` and `animate-tsa-float-soft` utilities in `index.css` and
   uses them on the gallery background images. If you apply a float to an
   ornament, it must be disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`. Check
   whether those existing utilities already handle that — if they don't, that's
   worth raising, though fixing it is out of scope here.

9. **Do not add ornament to the hero.** The hero is a full-bleed photo with a
   gradient and a headline; it's the one place on the site that's already doing
   enough. Leave it.

## Out of Scope

- Modifying the ornament components themselves (go back to W38.2.1 if they need changing)
- Ornamenting the footer or gallery page
- Changing section copy, layout or spacing beyond what the ornaments need
- Building new ornaments

## Hints

- Mobile is where ornament goes wrong. A divider that's elegant at 1440px can eat
  a third of a 375px viewport. Responsive utilities, or `hidden sm:block` on the
  heavier pieces, are fine answers.
- For the watermark, `overflow-hidden` on the section and a negative offset on
  the SVG gives you the bleeding-off-the-edge effect cleanly.
- Test the watermark opacity on a real phone screen outdoors if you can. What
  reads as subtle on a bright laptop display can be invisible in practice.
- The About section has GSAP `ScrollTrigger` pinning on its cards. If you add
  elements inside the pinned cards you may affect measured heights — add
  decoration to the section *container* rather than inside a pinned card, and
  call `ScrollTrigger.refresh()` if things drift.
- Restraint scores better than volume in review. Three well-placed ornaments beat
  ten.
