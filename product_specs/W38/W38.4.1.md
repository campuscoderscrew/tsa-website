## Objective

Make the gallery page look like part of the TSA website. Right now it looks like
a different product.

## Background

The homepage is warm: sand background (`#f0ebe6`), cream cards, crimson and navy
sections, gold accents, rounded shapes, playful rotation. It's festive.

The gallery page is `#141414` — near-black — edge to edge. The header is a
translucent dark bar. The masonry tiles sit on `white/5` with `white/10` rings.
There's a small "Gallery" chip in `white/10`. It's a competent dark photo
gallery and it shares **no** visual language with the rest of the site.

The transition makes it worse: `GalleryTransitionProvider` runs a deliberate
animated route change from the warm homepage straight into the dark page. The
polish of the transition draws attention to the mismatch.

Three specific things to fix:

1. `#141414` is a color used nowhere else on the site.
2. The header chip and back button are generic dark-UI patterns, not TSA's.
3. The page has no heading — you arrive with no title and no context.

## Specification

1. **Choose a surface that belongs to the brand.** Two defensible directions:

   - **Dark, on-brand.** Keep a dark gallery — photos do look good on dark — but
     use the deep navy from the palette instead of neutral near-black. Instantly
     more coherent, small diff.
   - **Light, matching the homepage.** Sand background, cream tiles, navy text.
     More consistent with the rest of the site; more work, and photos can lose
     punch.

   Try both. Screenshot both. Pick one and defend it in two sentences in the PR.
   **This decision is the substance of this spec** — the implementation is the
   easy half.

2. **Add a page heading.** Use the same eyebrow-label + heading pattern the
   homepage sections use, so the gallery reads as the same site. Something like
   an eyebrow reading "Gallery" and a heading naming what's here. The header bar
   currently has a "Gallery" chip that's decorative only; a real `<h1>` is both a
   design and an accessibility improvement — this page has **no h1 at all** right
   now.

3. **Restyle the back button and header bar** with brand colors. The back button
   works well and the behavior is right — `transitionTo("/")` runs the reverse
   route transition rather than a hard navigation, which is deliberate. Keep the
   behavior, change the skin.

4. **Keep the masonry layout.** The CSS-columns masonry (1 col → 2 at `sm` → 3
   at `lg`) is working. Don't rebuild it. You may adjust gaps, radii and ring
   colors to match the brand.

5. **Check the route transition against your new colors.**
   `GalleryTransitionProvider` has a crimson (`#9B1B30`) stroke baked into the
   transition artwork. Whatever surface you pick has to look right with crimson
   sweeping across it. Read that file before you choose. Changing that color is
   in scope; a *structural* change to the transition is not — stop and raise it
   rather than expanding this spec.

6. **Mind the safe areas.** The page uses `env(safe-area-inset-top)` and
   `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` for notched phones. Preserve that padding — it's
   easy to drop while restructuring, and the failure only shows up on a real
   iPhone.

7. **Don't touch alt text or captions.** That's W38.4.2 and it's deliberately
   separated so these two specs don't conflict.

## Hints

- Test the transition by actually clicking through it several times, not by
  loading `/gallery` directly. The two paths exercise different code.
- `columns-*` masonry orders items top-to-bottom per column, not left-to-right.
  That's inherent to CSS columns, and it's why item 2 appears below item 1 rather
  than beside it. Not a bug; don't "fix" it here.
- If you go light: photos on a light background usually need a subtle border or
  shadow to keep their edges, especially photos that are themselves bright.
- If you go dark: the deep navy is *much* warmer than `#141414`. Check that white
  text still hits AA against it — navy is lighter than near-black and contrast
  drops.
- The gallery currently shows nine images including three video poster frames.
  Note that for W38.4.2; don't change it here.
