## Objective

Let a visitor get to know a board member — major, year, fun facts — without
leaving the page, and make it work for mouse, touch and keyboard users alike.

## Background

W38.3.2 got name and role onto the card. There's more in the data than fits
there: major, year, and the fun facts that give TSA's board cards their warmth.
On Instagram those are the best part — favorite snack, hobbies, a favorite show.
They're what makes the board look like people you'd want to hang out with rather
than a staff directory.

The card already has a rich hover state (GSAP inertia, scale, shadow). You're
layering an information reveal on top of interaction that already exists, which
is exactly where hover effects usually go wrong.

**The trap:** hover-only reveals are invisible on touch, where there is no hover.
Roughly half of this site's visitors will be on a phone, most of them arriving
from the Instagram bio link. If the fun facts only appear on `:hover`, most
people will never see them. Solve for touch and keyboard from the start, not as
a follow-up.

## Specification

1. **Reveal the extra detail on interaction.** Minimum: major, year (if present),
   and fun facts. Pull it from `BOARD_MEMBERS` — do not hardcode.

2. **Pick an interaction model and make it work everywhere.** Two reasonable
   approaches:

   - **Overlay on the card.** Detail fades in over the photo on hover/focus, and
     on tap for touch. Compact; constrained by the ~200px card.
   - **Expanding panel or dialog.** Clicking a card opens a larger view.
     More room; more state to manage.

   Either is acceptable. State which you chose and why in the PR. If you go with
   a dialog, `@base-ui/react` is already a dependency and worth looking at before
   you build focus management by hand.

3. **Touch must work.** Decide the behavior explicitly: tap to reveal, tap
   elsewhere to dismiss, is a common answer. Test on a real phone or in devtools
   device mode with touch emulation on — hovering with a mouse in a narrow
   viewport does **not** test this.

4. **Keyboard must work.** The card has to be reachable by <kbd>Tab</kbd>, the
   detail has to appear on `:focus-visible`, and <kbd>Esc</kbd> has to dismiss it
   if it's dismissible. If cards become interactive they need to be real
   `<button>`s or have proper `role` and `tabIndex` — a `<div>` with an `onClick`
   is invisible to keyboard users.

   Tab through the whole grid before you open the PR. All fourteen.

5. **Screen readers need the detail too.** If content is visually hidden until
   hover, make sure it's either in the DOM and announced, or properly wired with
   `aria-expanded` / `aria-controls`. Don't hide it with `display: none` and
   assume that's fine.

6. **Don't fight the inertia effect.** The GSAP `quickTo` handlers respond to
   `pointermove`, `pointerdown`, `pointerup` and `pointerleave` on the card. Your
   overlay will sit inside that element and can swallow those events, which will
   make the inertia die whenever the overlay is up. `pointer-events-none` on the
   overlay (with `pointer-events-auto` on anything genuinely interactive inside
   it) is usually the fix. Verify the inertia still works after your change.

7. **Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.** If the reveal animates, it should be
   instant (or a plain opacity fade) under reduced motion. The About section
   already shows the pattern with `gsap.matchMedia()` — go read it.

8. **Keep it fast.** Fourteen cards each with their own listeners and animation.
   Don't add a per-card `setInterval`, a `ResizeObserver`, or a scroll listener.
   If you find yourself needing one, the design is too complex for this spec.

9. **Handle missing data.** `funFacts`, `major` and `year` are optional in the
   type. Some members will have none of them. A card with no extra detail should
   either not offer the interaction at all or degrade cleanly — an empty overlay
   that says nothing is worse than no overlay.

## Hints

- `:focus-visible` rather than `:focus` — otherwise the overlay flashes on every
  mouse click, which looks broken.
- Tailwind's `group` / `group-hover:` / `group-focus-within:` variants cover a lot
  of this declaratively, without extra React state. `group-focus-within` in
  particular handles the keyboard case almost for free.
- Touch devices synthesize a `mouseover` on first tap in some browsers. This
  causes the classic "have to tap twice" bug. `@media (hover: hover)` lets you
  scope hover-only behavior to devices that actually have a pointer.
- The site already uses this idea — `BoardMembersSection.tsx` matches
  `(min-width: 768px)` to decide whether to attach pointer handlers at all. Same
  problem, adjacent solution.
- Framer Motion is a dependency and `AnimatePresence` handles enter/exit cleanly
  if you go the dialog route. `UpcomingEvents.tsx` uses it — read that first.
- Budget time for testing, not building. The build is maybe 25 minutes; hover,
  touch and keyboard across fourteen cards is the rest.
